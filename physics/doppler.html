Doppler Shift

The major reflector in Doppler is the red blood cell.

Explore direction of flow

Select a direction to see the expected frequency shift and common waveform placement.

Positive Doppler shift
Blood moving toward the transducer produces an increase in frequency and is typically displayed above the baseline, depending on settings.

Clinical associations from the handout

Arteries: described as supplying organs and tissues with oxygenated blood, commonly shown above the baseline.

Veins: described as draining organs and tissues back toward the heart, commonly shown below the baseline.

Exception: the main portal vein supplies blood to the liver, so its flow is commonly shown above the baseline.

Zero shift: If blood is not moving toward or away from the transducer, there is no Doppler shift. The handout uses an occlusive DVT as an example of absent color fill and waveform because blood flow is blocked.

At 90°, the beam is perpendicular to flow. Cosine 90° = 0, so no Doppler shift is detected.

Key cosine values

0° = 1: maximum positive Doppler shift.

180° = -1: maximum negative Doppler shift.

90° = 0: no Doppler shift.

60° = 0.5: half of the true velocity is represented.

Scanning takeaway

The handout emphasizes aiming for 60° or less and avoiding vessels that lie flat across the screen.

Flat = perpendicular = no Doppler shift.

Types of Doppler

Review what each mode tells you.

Color Doppler

Overview of flow

  • Presence of flow
  • Direction of flow
  • Flow style, such as laminar or turbulent
  • Average or mean velocities
Pulsed Wave

Specific velocity data

  • Presence of flow
  • Direction of flow
  • Flow style
  • Specific velocities, including PSV and EDV
Power Doppler

Sensitive detection

  • Presence of flow only
  • More sensitive than Color and PW Doppler
  • Useful for very low flow and deep vessels
  • Can help distinguish trickle flow from total occlusion

Pulsed Wave

Cons: Nyquist limit, aliasing, and less effective for very high velocities.

Continuous Wave

What are its key advantages and limitations?

Continuous Wave

Pros: no Nyquist limit, no aliasing, and excellent measurement of high velocities.

Cons: no anatomic image and no depth information because of range ambiguity.

Aliasing

Review what it looks like, what causes it, and how to correct it.

Color Doppler aliasing

Appears as a mosaic of colors within the vessel.

It may indicate that the scale is set too low for that vessel, or it may represent turbulent flow.

PW Doppler aliasing

Waveform peaks are cut off, wrap around the baseline, and appear in the opposite direction.
