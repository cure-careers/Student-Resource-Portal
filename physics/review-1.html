Sound vs. Ultrasound

Build the core definitions first.

Core Definitions

Sound wave: A series of compressions and rarefactions.
Mechanical wave: Requires a medium and cannot travel through a vacuum.
Longitudinal wave: Particle motion is parallel to propagation. Ultrasound in tissue is longitudinal.
Transverse wave: Particle motion is perpendicular to propagation.

Wave Parameters and Definitions

Connect each parameter to its definition and relationship.

Frequency

Cycles per second. Measured in hertz.

f = 1 ÷ T

Period

Time required for one cycle.

T = 1 ÷ f

Wavelength

Distance of one cycle.

λ = c ÷ f

Key Definitions

  • Hertz: cycles per second
  • Cycle: one compression plus one rarefaction
  • Propagation speed: determined by density and stiffness of the medium
  • Key relationship: c = f × λ

Frequency and Period Examples

1 MHz corresponds to 1 μs.

10 MHz corresponds to 0.1 μs.

If propagation speed increases in a given medium while frequency remains constant, wavelength increases.

SPI Must-Know Equations and Constants

Use the expandable groups and calculators for active review.

Core Wave Equations

  • c = f × λ
  • T = 1/f
  • f = 1/T

Pulsed Parameters

  • PD = period × number of cycles per pulse
  • SPL = number of cycles per pulse × wavelength
  • PRF = 1/PRP
  • PRP = 1/PRF
  • DF = PD/PRP

Intensity

  • Power ∝ amplitude²
  • Intensity = power/area
  • SPPA × DF = SPTA
  • SPTA ÷ SP/SA factor = SATA

Impedance, Depth, and Nyquist

  • Z = density × propagation speed
  • Depth = speed × time of flight ÷ 2
  • 13 μs round trip = 1 cm depth
  • Nyquist limit = PRF/2
